\title{Beyond WER: Diagnosing Hallucination-Like Failures in Generative ASR Under Acoustic Stress}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Word error rate (WER) measures how inaccurate an automatic speech recognition
(ASR) output is, but not how it fails. Under weak acoustic evidence, generative
ASR systems may produce ordinary recognition errors, repetitive decoding,
generic decoder defaults, cross-utterance output collapse, or fluent text that
is weakly supported by the signal. Rather than proposing another scalar
hallucination score, we characterize failures with complementary measures of
lexical error, LM-relative plausibility, within-output repetition,
cross-utterance output concentration, and independent acoustic support. We
evaluate raw Whisper-large-v3, a Common-Voice-adapted Whisper-large-v3, and
SeamlessM4T-v2-Large under matched acoustic stress. The systems exhibit sharply
different severe-noise regimes: raw Whisper collapses toward a few generic
outputs, adapted Whisper maintains a high LM-relative ratio while becoming
strongly concentrated, and SeamlessM4T combines a high ratio with substantial
repetition. External validation on 4,990 human-annotated HALAS predictions
shows that Rep34 occurs in 69.0\% of human-labeled looping outputs but only 1.8\%
of non-looping outputs. Targeted intervention experiments further show that
anti-repetition decoding substantially improves the repetition-heavy SeamlessM4T
failure but has little effect on raw Whisper's dominant collapse. The results
support treating hallucination-like ASR failure as heterogeneous failure
regimes whose signatures have different operational meaning.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
automatic speech recognition, hallucination, robustness, acoustic stress,
evaluation, decoding failure
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Large generative ASR systems combine acoustic evidence with strong learned
linguistic priors. These priors are useful while the speech signal remains
informative, but when acoustic evidence becomes insufficient they can dominate
decoding and produce fluent yet weakly grounded text. Recent work has reported
hallucination-like ASR behavior under non-speech input, distribution shift,
disfluency, and challenging recording conditions
\cite{koenecke2024careless,atwany2025lost,baranski2025investigation,sridhar25_interspeech,imai-etal-2025-evaluating}.

A central difficulty is that WER does not identify the \emph{type} of failure.
Two hypotheses can have similarly catastrophic WER while representing very
different behaviors: one may be a short generic decoder default, another a
repeated phrase, and another a fluent sentence with little acoustic support.
These distinctions matter because they imply different intervention targets.
A repetition penalty cannot fix a failure dominated by cross-utterance mode
collapse, and a decoder-level constraint cannot restore information that is no
longer present in the signal.

We therefore ask a focused question: \emph{when acoustic evidence deteriorates,
do generative ASR systems fail in the same way?} We apply a shared
acoustic-stress protocol to three systems spanning two model families and two
Whisper model states, then characterize the resulting behavior with
complementary failure signatures. Severe acoustic uncertainty does not induce a
universal regime: Raw Whisper primarily collapses to a few generic defaults;
adapted Whisper maintains a high LM-relative ratio while becoming increasingly
concentrated; and SeamlessM4T combines a high ratio with substantial
within-output repetition.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we show that matched acoustic
degradation produces qualitatively different cross-system failure regimes even
when lexical error is similarly severe. Second, we externally validate one
structural axis on HALAS \cite{baranski2026halas}: Rep34 strongly tracks
human-annotated looping on naturally occurring speech errors, whereas
cross-utterance recurrence does not behave as a generic hallucination detector.
Third, we show that failure signatures have operational value: the same targeted
intervention behaves very differently depending on which component dominates.
The contribution is therefore the identification and validation of distinct
failure regimes, not a new universal hallucination metric.

\section{Related Work}
Hallucination-like behavior in ASR has been studied from several complementary
angles. Recent analyses report fabricated, repeated, or weakly grounded
transcriptions under non-speech input, distribution shift, disfluent speech,
and difficult acoustic conditions
\cite{koenecke2024careless,atwany2025lost,baranski2025investigation,sridhar25_interspeech,imai-etal-2025-evaluating,Zhang2024}.
HALAS \cite{baranski2026halas} complements synthetic-stress studies with
human span-level annotations of hallucination, looping, and looping
hallucination on naturally occurring Earnings22 speech. These works establish
that severe ASR errors can be reliability failures rather than ordinary lexical
mistakes.

WER remains the standard ASR metric, yet it conflates substitutions, deletions,
insertions, repetition, and unsupported generation. Complementary metrics based
on semantic similarity, information content, or fluency can expose additional
structure \cite{martindale-etal-2019-identifying,ERRATTAHI201832,papineni-etal-2002-bleu,lin-2004-rouge,mutton-etal-2007-gleu}. Recent work such as SHALLOW
\cite{Koudounas2025SHALLOW} likewise motivates multi-axis evaluation of ASR
hallucination. Our contribution is complementary rather than another metric
bundle: we study how observable diagnostics co-vary into distinct
\emph{failure regimes} under matched degradation, validate the repetition axis
against independent human labels, and test whether those regimes predict how
simple interventions behave.

Perturbation-based evaluation is a natural way to expose such regimes. Noise
injection and robustness studies show that input degradation can change not
only the amount but also the structure of ASR error
\cite{Fetic2021TopicMR,morales2007adding,Iwamoto2022HowBA}. Meanwhile,
mitigation work spans rescoring, contextual biasing, error correction,
sequence-level training, robust acoustic training, post-processing, and decoder
adaptation
\cite{shivakumar_li_knight_georgiou_2019,fang-etal-2022-non,Sudo2024ContextualizedAS,udagawa2024robustasrerrorcorrection,rao2021mean,Park2019SpecAugmentAS,baranski2025investigation,wang25b_interspeech}.
Rather than proposing another mitigation method, we use simple targeted
interventions as probes of whether the diagnosed failure dimensions are
behaviorally meaningful.

\section{Diagnostic Framework}
\subsection{Multi-axis failure profile}
We characterize each hypothesis along five complementary axes. \textbf{Lexical
error} is measured by WER using one frozen normalization pipeline. We apply
Unicode NFKC normalization and lowercasing, remove model control tokens, expand
unambiguous English contractions, treat punctuation and hyphens as token
boundaries, and exclude invalid placeholder references. \textbf{LM-relative
plausibility} uses Qwen3-0.6B as the primary evaluator and GPT-2 as a robustness
check. For LM $m$, let $s_m(z)=\exp(-\overline{\mathrm{NLL}}_m(z))$. We report
$P_m=\min(1,s_m(\hat y)/(s_m(y)+10^{-8}))$, where $y$ is the reference. Thus
$P_m$ is an evaluation-only, reference-normalized fluency ratio rather than a
reference-free detector. \textbf{Within-output repetition} is the incidence of
at least one repeated trigram or four-gram (Rep34). \textbf{Cross-utterance
concentration} is the fraction of a condition mapped to the most frequent
normalized hypothesis (Top-1 mass). Finally, \textbf{acoustic support} is
measured with an independent wav2vec2-CTC recognizer using
target-token-normalized negative log-likelihood of the generated hypothesis.

No single axis is treated as hallucination ground truth. In particular,
repetition and output concentration remain separate from the LM-relative ratio:
a short generic default can be highly concentrated while receiving a low ratio,
whereas a fluent unsupported hypothesis can receive a high ratio without
belonging to a dominant output mode.

\subsection{Operational hallucination-like signature}
To trace high-error/high-LM-ratio behavior, for LM evaluator $m$ we use
\begin{equation}
H_m^{\mathrm{diag}}(x)=
\mathbb{1}[\mathrm{WER}(x)>\tau_W]
\mathbb{1}[P_m(x)>\tau_m],
\end{equation}
where $\tau_W$ and $\tau_m$ are estimated from the corresponding model's clean
development distribution and frozen before stress evaluation. We additionally
report $H_m^{\mathrm{strict}}$, replacing the first condition with
$\mathrm{WER}>0.5$. The clean diagnostic rate is a calibration reference, not
an estimate of hallucination prevalence. Qwen3 is reported in the main results;
GPT-2 is used to verify that the qualitative conclusions do not depend on one
LM evaluator.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Models and acoustic stress}
We evaluate three generative ASR systems on English Mozilla Common Voice 22.0
(2025-06-20): untouched \texttt{openai/whisper-large-v3} (Raw Whisper), the
same Whisper-large-v3 checkpoint with lightweight LoRA adaptation on English
Common Voice (Adapted Whisper), and untouched
\texttt{facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large}. Development and test manifests are
disjoint. Each cross-model condition contains the same 1,000 held-out test
utterance IDs across all three systems. The adapted model is additionally
evaluated with silence, leading silence, localized noise, reverberation,
speech-band noise, and full-utterance additive noise. The cross-model comparison
uses clean speech and full-utterance noise at amplitudes 0.50 and 0.75. Full
noise adds i.i.d. Gaussian waveform noise scaled by this absolute amplitude
before clipping to $[-1,1]$; the stress levels are not SNR-normalized. All
cross-model perturbations are generated deterministically using the same random
seed, ensuring matched noisy inputs across models. Model-specific thresholds
are calibrated on clean development outputs and frozen before test evaluation.

The severe conditions are diagnostic stress tests rather than a simulation of
one specific deployment environment. The broader battery establishes that not
all perturbations produce the same behavior: moderate or localized corruption
primarily increases recognition error, whereas the severe full-utterance
conditions expose decoder-dominated regimes after acoustic constraint has been
substantially weakened.

\subsection{External validation on HALAS}
To test whether structural failure signatures transfer beyond synthetic stress,
we evaluate them on the official HALAS test split \cite{baranski2026halas}.
HALAS provides human span annotations distinguishing hallucination, looping, and
looping hallucination on natural Earnings22 speech, together with corrected
references and model predictions. We analyze 4,990 predictions from seven ASR
systems (Whisper large-v2, large-v3, large-v3-turbo, CrisperWhisper, Canary,
Canary Flash, and Parakeet); Phi-4 and Granite are excluded because HALAS notes
that they were trained on Earnings22. No ASR inference is rerun. Rep34 and
exact-output recurrence are computed directly from the released predictions,
and WER uses the corrected references.

\subsection{Independent support and interventions}
We use \texttt{facebook/wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self} as an independent
wav2vec2-CTC acoustic-support model. Its summed CTC loss for the generated
hypothesis is normalized by the number of CTC target tokens. A reference-free
abstention rule emits a hypothesis only when
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{NLL}_{\mathrm{CTC}}(x,\hat y)}{|\hat y|}\leq \tau,
\end{equation}
with $\tau$ calibrated on disjoint stressed development data and frozen for test
evaluation. For the Raw-Whisper and SeamlessM4T analyses reported below, the
threshold is selected subject to at least 95\% clean-DEV coverage.

To test whether the diagnostic profile has decision utility, we evaluate
(1) anti-repetition decoding with a fixed repetition penalty of 1.2, set before
TEST and not tuned on TEST, and (2) a dominant-output rejection lexicon learned
only from stressed DEV while preserving at least 99\% clean-DEV coverage
(maximum 20 entries). These are deliberately simple probes targeting different
textual signatures; they are not proposed as state-of-the-art mitigation
methods.

\textbf{Raw Whisper: decoder-default collapse.} At noise 0.50, Top-1 mass is
46.2\%; at 0.75 it rises to 67.2\%. At the same time the Qwen3 LM-relative ratio
falls from 0.255 to 0.113 and $H_Q$ decreases from 21.5\% to 9.9\%. Inspection of
the frequency distribution shows that the dominant outputs are short generic
strings such as ``you'' and ``thank you''. Thus, worsening acoustics increases
output collapse while decreasing the LM-based diagnostic score. This is a
failure mode that a high-ratio criterion alone would miss.

\textbf{Adapted Whisper: high LM-relative ratio with concentration.} Under the
same stress protocol, the ratio remains high (0.917--0.957) and $H_Q$ reaches
86.9--93.3\%. Top-1 mass simultaneously rises from 27.0\% to 49.6\%. Relative to
raw Whisper, the adapted model therefore produces substantially higher
LM-relative ratios while still becoming concentrated on a restricted output
distribution. We describe this association as a model-state difference rather
than attributing it causally to fine-tuning.

\textbf{SeamlessM4T: high-ratio failure with substantial repetition.}
SeamlessM4T reaches WER 2.184--2.380 while the Qwen3 LM-relative ratio remains
0.971--0.977 and $H_Q$ exceeds 93\%. Unlike raw Whisper, Top-1 mass remains low
(6.4--7.7\%), but Rep34 rises to 27.5--28.6\%. The output distribution is
therefore comparatively diverse while containing substantial within-output
degeneration.

Bootstrap confidence intervals ($B=2{,}000$) confirm that the major contrasts
are not explained by test-set sampling. At noise 0.50, Rep34 95\% intervals are
1.2--2.9\% (Raw), 12.6--16.9\% (Adapted), and 24.8--30.4\% (Seamless); Top-1
intervals are 43.0--49.2\%, 24.2--29.7\%, and 5.5--7.9\%, respectively. Clean
WER intervals overlap across all three systems. GPT-2 scoring yields the same
qualitative separation between raw default collapse and the high-ratio
Adapted/Seamless regimes.

These results show why a single hallucination rate is insufficient. Loss of
acoustic constraint is common to the severe conditions, but the resulting
textual phenotype differs substantially across systems and model states.

\subsection{External human validation of the repetition axis}
On HALAS, Rep34 is present in 69.0\% of human-labeled looping outputs but only
1.76\% of non-looping outputs (Fisher odds ratio 123.8,
$p=6.56\times10^{-53}$). As a descriptive single-axis discriminator, Rep34
achieves ROC-AUC 0.836 for human looping. The phenotype breakdown is consistent:
Rep34 occurs in only 2.43\% of ordinary hallucination outputs, versus 57.7\% of
looping outputs and 90.0\% of looping-hallucination outputs. Thus Rep34 transfers
to natural speech as a specific repetition/looping signature rather than a
generic hallucination score.

Cross-utterance recurrence does not show the same transfer. Exact-output
recurrence is only borderline associated with human hallucination
(odds ratio 1.34, $p=0.053$), and the continuous recurrence frequency has
ROC-AUC 0.508. WER remains informative for hallucination itself (ROC-AUC 0.761),
but does not identify the failure type. We therefore interpret concentration as
a stress-induced decoder-collapse phenotype, while Rep34 has direct external
support as a human-recognizable looping component.

\subsection{Independent acoustic support}
The independent CTC score provides a separate check on whether hypotheses remain
supported by the waveform under severe stress. When used as a frozen abstention
gate, it retains 96.5\% of clean Raw-Whisper outputs and 97.0\% of clean
SeamlessM4T outputs. At noise 0.50, coverage falls to 0.1\% and 0.2\%, and at
0.75 to 0\% for both models; all strict Qwen3- and GPT-2-defined cases are
therefore rejected under these extreme conditions. This should not be read as a
near-perfect detector: at severe stress the gate is intentionally conservative
and assigns insufficient support to almost every hypothesis. Its value is as an
independent acoustic-grounding check, not as error correction.

For Raw Whisper, anti-repetition decoding reduces Rep34 from 2.0\% to 0.2\% but
barely changes WER, strict $H_Q$, or Top-1 mass. It removes a secondary symptom
without addressing the dominant cross-utterance collapse. For SeamlessM4T, the
same intervention reduces Rep34 from 27.5\% to 3.9\% and WER from 2.184 to
1.822. Yet strict $H_Q$ remains 89.2\% and Top-1 mass increases from 6.4\% to
14.5\%. Suppressing repetition therefore improves one diagnosed component while
leaving the high-error/high-ratio signature largely unresolved and shifting the
output distribution toward greater concentration.

The dominant-output lexicon shows the complementary effect. It rejects 70.3\%
of Raw-Whisper outputs but captures only 4.7\% of strict $H_Q$ cases because many
of the dominant generic defaults do not exceed the Qwen3 ratio threshold. On
SeamlessM4T it rejects 58.3\% and captures 61.8\% of strict $H_Q$ cases. The
intervention results therefore validate the diagnostic decomposition: different
metrics correspond to different observable failure components, and improving
one axis need not resolve the others.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
Severe acoustic uncertainty does not induce one universal generative-ASR failure
mode. Under the same stress protocol, raw Whisper primarily maps acoustically
different utterances to a few generic decoder defaults; adapted Whisper preserves
a high LM-relative ratio while becoming strongly concentrated; and SeamlessM4T
produces similarly high-ratio output with substantial within-output repetition.
WER alone cannot distinguish these regimes, while the LM-relative ratio alone
would miss raw Whisper's strongest collapse.

The HALAS validation shows that at least one structural axis transfers beyond
synthetic stress: Rep34 is strongly associated with independently
human-annotated looping on natural speech. The negative recurrence result is
equally informative: cross-utterance concentration should not be interpreted as
a universal hallucination detector, but as a decoder-collapse signature exposed
under the severe matched-stress conditions. Together with the intervention
probes, this supports a failure-regime interpretation rather than compressing
all outputs into one scalar hallucination category.

The intervention probes also show why this distinction matters operationally.
Anti-repetition decoding is effective when repetition is prominent, but does
little for decoder-default collapse and does not by itself restore acoustic
grounding. Independent CTC support provides a phenotype-agnostic abstention
signal when the independent acoustic model assigns little support to the
hypothesis.

The current study is limited to three model states from two generative ASR
families and deliberately severe synthetic stress. HALAS provides external
human validation on natural Earnings22 errors, but does not contain human
judgments for our exact stressed Common Voice outputs. The operational
high-WER/high-LM-ratio criterion remains evaluation-only and
reference-normalized, and the acoustic-support gate becomes near-total
abstention once the signal is overwhelmed. Broader model families, realistic
environmental noise, and direct human grounding judgments on matched stressed
outputs remain important future work.
